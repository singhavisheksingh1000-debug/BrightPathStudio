Pregnancy Journal Ideas You'll Treasure Forever

A pregnancy journal does not have to be perfect. The most valuable pages are often the ordinary ones: what made you laugh, what you were wondering about, what you were preparing and how you felt on a particular Tuesday.

Milestones to record

  • The moment you found out and how you felt.
  • When you shared the news and people's reactions.
  • First appointments, scans and memorable updates.
  • Changes you noticed in your energy, routines and everyday life.
  • Special dates, celebrations and meaningful firsts.

Prompts about feelings

Try prompts such as: “Today I am excited about…”, “Something I want to remember about this season is…”, “A fear I am learning to hold gently is…” and “One thing I hope my future child knows about this time is…”.

Letters to your baby

Write short letters at different stages. You can describe the world around you, what you are learning, what you hope to teach and what you already love. These pages can become some of the most personal parts of the journal.

Practical memories

Record nursery ideas, name lists, favourite meals, books you read, songs you listened to, gifts, baby-shower memories and preparations. These details help tell the story of everyday life, not just major milestones.

Include other voices

Invite a partner, sibling, grandparent or close friend to write a page. Ask what they are looking forward to, what they remember about you at this stage or what they hope for the baby.

Make the journal sustainable

Do not require yourself to write every day. A weekly page, monthly reflection or occasional photo-and-caption entry is enough. Consistency matters less than capturing moments honestly.

Note: A journal is a memory-keeping tool, not a substitute for medical care. For pregnancy questions or concerns, follow advice from your qualified healthcare professional.
