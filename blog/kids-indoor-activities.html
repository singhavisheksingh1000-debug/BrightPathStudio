50 Indoor Activities for Kids

Rainy days, school breaks and “I'm bored” afternoons do not need a complicated setup. The best indoor activities use things you already have and offer a clear starting point.

Creative activities

  1. Make a paper-collage creature.
  2. Design a family flag.
  3. Create a cardboard-box city.
  4. Make bookmarks.
  5. Draw a comic strip.
  6. Build a paper crown.
  7. Create a homemade board game.
  8. Decorate a recycled jar.
  9. Make a paper puppet.
  10. Design a dream bedroom.

Learning through play

  1. Create a mini treasure hunt with clues.
  2. Sort household objects by size or color.
  3. Make a simple word-search puzzle.
  4. Practice spelling with letter cards.
  5. Build a shape challenge with paper.
  6. Measure ingredients during a simple recipe.
  7. Make a weather chart.
  8. Write five facts about a favourite animal.
  9. Play “guess the object” using clues.
  10. Invent a new animal and describe its habitat.

Movement & energy

  1. Have a music freeze dance.
  2. Create an indoor obstacle course.
  3. Play balloon volleyball.
  4. Try animal-walk races.
  5. Make a movement dice game.
  6. Have a balance challenge.
  7. Play follow-the-leader.
  8. Do a short kids yoga session.
  9. Try a sock-basket toss.
  10. Make a hallway bowling game with soft objects.

Pretend play

  1. Open a pretend restaurant.
  2. Run a toy hospital.
  3. Build a blanket campsite.
  4. Play post office.
  5. Create a pretend travel agency.
  6. Make a puppet theatre.
  7. Play grocery store.
  8. Run a toy news station.
  9. Set up a classroom.
  10. Invent a superhero mission.

Quiet & independent activities

  1. Make a reading nook.
  2. Complete a printable puzzle.
  3. Color a themed page.
  4. Write a short story.
  5. Make a gratitude list.
  6. Build a small LEGO or block model.
  7. Complete a drawing prompt.
  8. Make a memory box.
  9. Create a “things I want to learn” list.
  10. Choose three favourite activities and make a personal activity menu.

For younger children, adapt materials and supervise activities involving small pieces, scissors, food or household items. The goal is not to keep children entertained every minute; it is to give them inviting ideas they can return to.
