The Ultimate Pregnancy Journal Guide: What to Write From the First Positive Test to Baby's First Year

Pregnancy can feel like one long collection of tiny moments: the first positive test, the first appointment, the first time your bump changes, a late-night thought, a name you suddenly love, and eventually the day you meet your baby. A pregnancy journal gives those moments somewhere to live.

This guide shows you what to write in a pregnancy journal from the first positive test through baby's first year. You do not need perfect handwriting, long entries or a daily routine. You simply need a way to capture the details you may want to remember later.

Why keep a pregnancy journal?

A pregnancy journal is more than a list of appointments or a place to record a due date. It can become a personal timeline of the transition into parenthood. Writing down ordinary details can make the final keepsake feel more complete because major milestones are surrounded by the small memories that give them context.

You can record emotions, hopes, funny moments, preparations, family reactions, photographs and messages to your future child. A baby journal can also become a place for a partner or family member to contribute their perspective.

The goal is not to create a perfect book. The goal is to create something that sounds like you.

What to write after the first positive test

The first positive test is one of the easiest moments to remember emotionally and one of the easiest to forget in detail. Start with a simple first entry while the memory is fresh.

Record the moment

  • When you took the test.
  • Where you were.
  • What you noticed first.
  • How you reacted.
  • Who you told first.
  • What you were thinking that day.
  • One thing you immediately wanted to remember.
Try this prompt:

“The moment I realized I was pregnant, I…”

“The first person I wanted to tell was…”

“If I could save one feeling from today forever, it would be…”

Pregnancy journal ideas by trimester

Breaking your journal into stages makes a big project feel manageable. You can use the beginning of each trimester as a reset point and then add entries whenever something meaningful happens.

First trimester: the beginning of the story

Capture the earliest part of the journey while everything is new. Consider writing about how you shared the news, your first appointment memories, your early hopes, and the changes you noticed in everyday life.

  • How you felt about becoming a parent.
  • First reactions from your partner or family.
  • Early pregnancy milestones you want to remember.
  • Foods, routines or little things that became memorable.
  • Questions or hopes you were carrying into the next stage.

Second trimester: growing, preparing and imagining

This stage can be a wonderful place to capture the shift from “this is happening” to “we are preparing to meet our baby.” Record moments that feel personal rather than trying to document everything.

  • How your perspective changed.
  • Baby-name ideas and the stories behind them.
  • Nursery inspiration.
  • Baby shower memories.
  • Special gifts and who gave them.
  • Thoughts about the kind of parent you hope to be.

Third trimester: getting ready to meet your baby

The final stage is full of preparation and anticipation. Create pages for the things you want your future self and child to know about this time.

  • What you are most excited about.
  • What you are preparing at home.
  • People who supported you.
  • Last quiet moments before baby's arrival.
  • Messages you want your baby to read one day.
  • What you hope to remember about the final weeks.

Pregnancy journal prompts that make writing easier

If a blank page makes you freeze, use prompts. You can answer one question in a few sentences and stop. There is no requirement to write an essay.

  1. What made me smile today?
  2. What is one thing I want to remember about this week?
  3. What am I looking forward to?
  4. What surprised me about pregnancy?
  5. What has my partner done that I appreciate?
  6. What has my family said that I want to remember?
  7. What song, book, meal or place reminds me of this stage?
  8. What have I learned about myself?
  9. What do I hope my baby knows about how loved they were before birth?
  10. What ordinary moment from today might become a precious memory later?

Photos, keepsakes and everyday details

Words are only one part of a memory book. Add photographs and small details that help tell the story visually. Consider including ultrasound photos where appropriate, bump photos, nursery details, celebration photos, handwritten notes, cards, or pictures of meaningful places and people.

Keep captions simple. Write the date, what was happening and why the moment mattered. A single sentence can make a photo much more meaningful years later.

Caption formula:

Date + what happened + how it felt.

Example: “August 18 — We finally finished the little corner of the nursery. It made everything feel real.”

Letters to your baby

Letters can become some of the most emotional pages in a pregnancy journal. You can write one letter at the beginning, one near the end of pregnancy, and more whenever you feel inspired.

Tell your baby about the world they are arriving into, the people waiting to meet them, the hopes you have for their life and the things you already love about them. You can also write honestly about the uncertainty of becoming a parent. Those real feelings can make the keepsake more personal.

Letter prompts

  • “Before I met you, I wanted you to know…”
  • “The people already waiting for you are…”
  • “I hope you always remember…”
  • “One thing I am learning before you arrive is…”
  • “If I could give you one piece of advice for life, it would be…”

Birth story and the first days

After pregnancy comes a new chapter. When you are ready, record the story of your baby's arrival in whatever level of detail feels comfortable. Focus on the memories you personally want preserved.

  • The date and time your baby arrived.
  • Who was with you.
  • The first moment you saw or held your baby.
  • What you remember hearing, seeing or feeling.
  • Who you contacted first.
  • A first photo or meaningful keepsake.
  • Your first thoughts as a new parent.

There is no single “right” birth story. Your journal is a personal keepsake, so write it in your own voice and at your own pace.

What to record during baby's first year

If your pregnancy journal continues into baby's first year, create a simple monthly rhythm. You can record milestones, personality, favorite things and family memories without turning the process into another task on your list.

Month-by-month ideas

  • 1 month: first impressions, routines, tiny changes and favorite photos.
  • 2 months: expressions, sounds, comforting routines and family visits.
  • 3 months: new interests, smiles, interactions and everyday favorites.
  • 4 months: personality changes, favorite songs, toys or activities.
  • 5 months: new discoveries, family moments and memorable outings.
  • 6 months: half-year reflection, favorite memories and hopes for the next six months.
  • 7–9 months: new skills, favorite people, places and routines.
  • 10–11 months: growing independence, funny moments and changing interests.
  • 12 months: first birthday memories, favorite things, a letter to your one-year-old and a reflection on the year.

How to keep journaling without pressure

The best pregnancy journal routine is one you can actually maintain. Instead of promising yourself a long entry every day, choose a low-pressure system.

  • Weekly: write three things you want to remember.
  • Monthly: add a photo, a short reflection and a few favorite details.
  • Milestone-based: write whenever something important happens.
  • Partner pages: invite your partner to contribute occasionally.
  • Photo-first: start with a picture and add two or three sentences.

Missing a week does not mean you failed. Leave the page blank or write a short catch-up entry. A keepsake becomes meaningful because it is honest, not because every page is perfect.

Turn your memories into a lasting keepsake

Once you know what you want to record, a structured pregnancy journal can make the process much easier. Instead of deciding what to write every time you open a blank page, you can move through prompts, milestone pages, reflections and memory sections in a consistent order.
